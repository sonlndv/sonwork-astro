# Real planet imagery: credits and derivation

Planet textures for Sonwork's illustrative off-world scene. Source imagery is credited below under [NASA's media usage guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/); this site does not imply NASA endorsement.

The source SHA-256 values, rendering parameters and exact generated texture hashes are recorded in [`real-material-provenance.json`](./real-material-provenance.json). The generated imagery comprises `real-neptune.webp`, `real-saturn.webp`, `real-earth-morning.webp`, `real-earth-night.webp`, `real-moon-morning.webp` and `real-moon-night.webp`, with the additional day textures and visibility masks described below.

| Body | Source | Credit | What the site shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neptune | [PIA00046](https://science.nasa.gov/image-detail/amf-pia00046/) (Voyager 2 full disk) | NASA/JPL | The photograph itself, unmirrored and not re-lit: a circular matte fitted to the observed limb and a native-resolution crop. The colour is feature-enhanced; it is not exact true colour and not the current sky. |
| Saturn | [PIA05389, "Saturn in color"](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/saturn-in-color/) (Cassini, natural colour) | NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute | The photograph, converted losslessly from the original TIFF to RGB PNG, rotated 180 degrees as a whole (a rotation of the view, not a mirror) so its light agrees with Neptune's. Matte from the real rings and body, a ring annulus (outer ring edge down to an inner edge measured from the lit side of the photograph; the empty space inside it stays transparent) and the observed globe silhouette; detached specks removed; downscaled to 648x256. |
| Earth | [Blue Marble, SVS 2915](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/2915/) (2048 px map) | NASA Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio; Blue Marble: Reto Stockli, NASA Earth Observatory | An orthographic render of a satellite-based composite map (eastern hemisphere), lit by one declared key. It is a map render, not a photograph. |
| Moon | [CGI Moon Kit, SVS 4720](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/) (2k colour map) | NASA Scientific Visualization Studio | An orthographic near-side render of an LRO-derived, aesthetically processed colour map (documented polar fill), lit by the same key with a Lommel-Seeliger law. It is not an unmodified photograph. |

Two further generated files, `real-earth-visibility.webp` and `real-saturn-visibility.webp`, are alpha-only derivatives (black RGB; alpha is a constant fraction, 0.35 and 0.20, of the rendered Earth and Saturn alpha). They carry no imagery and exist only as fixed shape masks that keep those two bodies legible under the scene's inherited dimming. The Earth render also receives a fixed display tone after its physical shading (night: out = 185 x (in/255)^0.72; morning: out = 26 + 0.86 x in), a display grade rather than a different source, key or phase. None of this makes the Earth a photograph or true colour.

Daylight aerial perspective: the four light-theme textures (`real-neptune-morning.webp`, `real-saturn-morning.webp`, `real-earth-morning.webp`, `real-moon-morning.webp`) receive one fixed display grade after the physical shading or photograph extraction. In linear light (standard sRGB transfer) each colour channel becomes (1 - k) x source + k x the existing day sky colour (229, 233, 238), with a fixed strength k per body (Neptune 0.22, Earth 0.16, Moon 0.30, Saturn 0.30; recorded in `real-material-provenance.json`) so Neptune and the small Earth keep their blue and the grey Moon and Saturn sit back a little more. The mixed colour's chroma is then scaled about its own linear luminance by a fixed per-body gain (Neptune 2.4, Earth 3.6, Moon and Saturn 1; capped so no channel leaves its range), which keeps luminance unchanged and restores the source's hue, so Neptune's blue and Earth's ocean are not washed to grey; every pixel with alpha above 0 is graded, alpha is untouched, and fully transparent pixels stay black. The Neptune and Saturn day files come from the same photographic RGBA as `real-neptune.webp` and `real-saturn.webp`, which stay unchanged as the night materials, as do the Earth and Moon night renders and both visibility masks. The earlier Earth morning curve (26 + 0.86 x in) is no longer applied. This is a display grade, not a different light, phase or source; it does not make any body true colour or an astronomical view.

Graded display versions (Son-approved 2026-11-03): the eight displayed textures (`real-neptune.webp`, `real-neptune-morning.webp`, `real-earth-night.webp`, `real-earth-morning.webp`, `real-moon-night.webp`, `real-moon-morning.webp`, `real-saturn.webp`, `real-saturn-morning.webp`) are graded display versions of the same sources described above. `scripts/grade-planets.mjs` produces them from the previously served textures, kept byte-identical in `src/assets/planet-sources/` (a build input that no page references): the same shapes and the same pixel size with unchanged alpha, re-rendered offline in the mountains' palette with one light, a distance haze and a thin rim on the lit limb. The grade supersedes the per-body daylight haze and chroma gains recorded above (they remain in the history of the sources); it is a display grade, not true colour, a different light or a different source, and every credit above still applies. The two visibility masks are unchanged.

The scene is an illustrative off-world valley. Placing these bodies together in one sky is art direction, not an astronomical observation.
